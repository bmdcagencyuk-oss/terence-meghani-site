# Multi-CRM occupancy intelligence is feasible, but Stora-first

A universal, multi-CRM occupancy intelligence SaaS for UK self-storage looks feasible, and the competitive gap looks real, but the evidence is too thin to commit to a full build. **Stora is the only UK system with a public, versioned, OAuth-based API and webhooks, and the only vendor claiming a measurable UK share (over 25% of facilities, vendor-reported).** Amax Pulse's current design therefore covers the one system that is easiest to integrate. Storeganise is the next most accessible, with documented webhooks and operator-created API keys, but its Admin API documentation is gated. For StorMan, Kinnovis, Space Manager and SiteLink/Storable, the research found marketing claims of an API but no developer documentation, auth method, rate limit or field list. I found **no independent, vendor-agnostic occupancy and benchmarking product for UK operators**; every dashboard found is tied to its own CRM, and the nearest multi-system tools are revenue-management engines (Prorize, Veritec) and market-rate trackers (StorTrack). The recommended path is a vendor-neutral core with a personal-data-free canonical model, Stora and Storeganise as API adapters, and an operator-run CSV or scheduled-report ingestion route that covers every other system from day one. Commit further engineering only after the verification steps in the final section.

## Every finding rests on search summaries, not primary pages

The researchers' page fetching failed with DNS errors, so no vendor documentation, pricing page, primary industry report or regulator page was read in full. Every claim below comes from search-result summaries of vendor sites, trade press and software directories. Three consequences follow. First, "no API found" means "not found in search", not "does not exist". Second, most capability claims are vendor-sourced marketing. Third, the SSA UK 2025 report, Stora's industry reports and the ICO guidance were not read directly.

Confidence is flagged in the text as high, medium or low. In this report, **high** means two or more consistent sources, at least one independent of the vendor. **Medium** means a single credible or trade-press source. **Low** means vendor self-report, directory listing, competitor claim or my inference. Almost nothing here reaches high confidence on vendor capability. Several figures conflict between sources and are flagged where they appear: Stora's share (25% against a 2024 sample of nearly 20%), group occupancy against SSA occupancy, and Kinnovis API licensing.

## The UK market is fragmented, and only Stora publishes a share

The Self Storage Association UK (SSA UK) report published on 30 April 2025 ([SSA UK](https://ssauk.com/resource/self-storage-annual-report-2025.html)) reports about **1,780 internal stores, 1,127 of them "significant", run by about 1,480 operators**, with occupancy of **75.1% (down 1 point)** and floorspace up 7.2% to 64.3m sq ft ([SSA UK 2025 report](https://ssauk.com/publications/uk-annual-industry-report-2025.html)). These figures reached me via search summaries (medium confidence). The container sector adds 652 operators and 1,212 sites ([The Mover](https://www.themover.co.uk/news/2025/09/11/uk-container-self-storage-growing-strong-in-a-competitive-market)). At roughly 1.2 stores per operator, the base is heavily independent. My estimate, which is low confidence, is that the listed chains hold 25-30% of internal stores, leaving about 1,100 to 1,300 independent internal stores plus the container operators as the buyable segment.

Software share is the weakest part of the evidence. Stora, based in Belfast, says it supports **25% of UK facilities and 580+ UK sites** ([Stora 2025 report](https://stora.co/industry-report-2025)). Its 2024 report described a sample of nearly 20% of facilities ([Inside Self-Storage](https://www.insideselfstorage.com/suppliers-products/software-firm-stora-releases-uk-self-storage-industry-insights-2024-report)), and the figure may count sites loosely. Treat it as low confidence. My own share estimates (SiteLink/Storable 15-25%, StorMan 5-10%, a long tail of smaller systems 15-25%, bespoke or spreadsheet 20-35%) are guesses with overlapping ranges and no independent census behind them. Review counts on Capterra are US-skewed and do not measure UK share ([Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/directory/30606/self-storage/software)).

No public evidence names the software used by Big Yellow, Safestore, Shurgard, Lok'nStore, Access or Storage King. They are widely assumed to run bespoke or heavily customised systems, but that is unevidenced and should not be asserted. If true, these operators cannot be reached through a vendor API and need file-based ingestion or direct commercial agreements. The commercial context supports demand for better analytics: occupancy is softening while supply grows, and Cushman & Wakefield says revenue management and pricing discipline separate the strongest operators ([SSA UK slides](https://ssauk.com/resource/uk-annual-industry-report-update-slides.html)).

## No vendor-agnostic occupancy analytics exists for UK operators

The competitive field has four layers, and none is cross-CRM. The first is built-in reporting. Storable launched Business Intelligence on 22 October 2025 for Edge operators, with SiteLink support "coming in the future" ([Modern Storage Media](https://www.modernstoragemedia.com/news/storable-launches-business-intelligence-reporting-solution)). Storeganise sells an Analytics add-on with occupancy by unit or area, refreshed once daily ([Storeganise help](https://help.storeganise.com/article/423-introduction-to-the-analytics-dashboard)). Kinnovis and Self Storage Manager offer Power BI. One 2022 reviewer called Stora's reporting limited, a single dated data point ([Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.co.uk/software/210892/stora)). The second layer is revenue management. Prorize integrates with SiteLink and Storeganise and claims 90+ operators in 28 countries, a figure that conflicts with Storable's "over a dozen countries" ([Storeganise partner page](https://storeganise.com/partner/prorize)). Veritec syncs from SiteLink and storEDGE with no UK presence found ([Storable](https://www.storable.com/?p=12639)). These are partners or data consumers more than rivals.

The third layer is market intelligence. StorTrack Explorer covers the UK and 32 European countries with 12,000+ facilities ([Inside Self-Storage](https://www.insideselfstorage.com/suppliers-products/stortrack-expands-explorer-self-storage-market-analysis-platform-to-cover-europe-uk)), and Aggregate Intelligence sells rate data ([Aggregate Intelligence](https://aggregateintelligence.com/?p=4869)). This complements internal-KPI aggregation rather than competing with it. The fourth layer is benchmarking, which for UK operators means annual surveys from SSA UK and FEDESSA, or Stora's report drawn only from its own customers. Nothing offers live, cross-CRM peer comparison.

Two risks temper the white-space reading. Demand evidence is indirect: vendors repeatedly say operators work from spreadsheets and fragmented data ([Inside Self-Storage on storEDGE](https://www.insideselfstorage.com/suppliers-products/self-storage-technology-firm-storedge-releases-integrated-business-intelligence-tools)), but no operator post, survey or forum thread was found. And CRMs are closing the gap inside their own walled gardens, so the multi-CRM niche matters mainly to operators and investors running mixed systems after acquisitions. That scenario is plausible and unevidenced. Stora could also respond to a benchmarking product with its own, since it holds the largest customer sample.

## Build a canonical core fed by adapters, with files as the universal fallback

No unified-API vendor covers self storage. Merge's categories do not include it, and Propexo covers multifamily systems such as Yardi and AppFolio ([Merge](https://merge.dev/integrations), [Propexo docs](https://docs.propexo.com)). The adapter layer must therefore be built in-house, and Propexo's residents, leases, units, charges and payments model is a useful precedent. The design below is my inference, as no source specifies one.

**Canonical model.** Operator, Facility, UnitType and Unit (status, size, features, street rate, actual rate) form the core, with a daily unit-status snapshot as the primary record. Tenancy (pseudonymous ID, dates, rate) and Invoice (period, amount, status) are optional modules switched on by per-adapter capability flags such as supportsUnitSnapshot, supportsWebhooks, supportsInvoices and ingestionMode. Daily snapshots should be the source of truth, with webhooks used only to speed up freshness, because Storeganise payloads carry just an ID and need a follow-up fetch ([Storeganise webhooks](https://help.storeganise.com/article/19-webhooks)).

**Ingestion ladder.** Prefer an official API or webhook, then a vendor feed or partner export, then operator-scheduled CSV or Excel delivered by SFTP or email, then a read-only agent on any on-premise install, and only last RPA with the operator's own credentials. Scraping carries breach-of-contract and database-right exposure that UK courts have not tested for this case, and the available sources are law-firm marketing pages ([Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/is-web-scraping-legal-in-the-uk/)); take solicitor advice before any RPA. Occupancy analytics needs only a unit list with status, size and rate, so scheduled report export is often enough and avoids deep integration. Whether each UK vendor offers scheduled exports is unconfirmed.

**Data protection.** Tenant names and invoices are personal data, which makes the SaaS a processor for the operator and requires an Article 28 contract. Pseudonymised data stays personal data under the ICO's March 2025 anonymisation guidance ([SCL](https://www.scl.org/ico-publishes-anonymisation-guidance/)). Drop tenant fields at ingestion with a field allow-list, aggregate benchmarks to facility level (a single-unit site with dates may be identifiable), and take advice if the SaaS uses client data for its own benchmarking, which could make it a controller for that use. The ICO's controller and processor pages were not read, so this rests on general knowledge.

**Pricing.** The anchors are low: Stora is listed from £99 a month for the whole CRM ([Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/1011583/stora)), Storeganise from about $90 and Rentya from $25 ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/232660/Storeganise)). An add-on above roughly £50 to £100 per site a month will meet resistance in my judgement. Price per operator or per portfolio, tiered by unit count, with any vendor API fee passed through. My rough ceiling for small-operator revenue is **£1.5m to £5m ARR** (2,500 to 3,000 sites at £600 to £1,800 a year), a low-confidence estimate with no UK willingness-to-pay data behind it.

**Integration priority.** Provisional order, subject to fingerprinting results: first, Stora, which is built and needs only its field coverage verified. Second, Storeganise, because it is the only other system with confirmed webhooks and operator-created API keys, once the Admin API documentation is obtained. Third, SiteLink/Storable, which probably carries a large UK installed base, with a likely API fee and a native BI product that will reach SiteLink eventually. Fourth, StorMan and Kinnovis, which have UK footprints and claimed APIs but need vendor conversations; Kinnovis already offers Power BI, which weakens the pitch there. Fifth, Space Manager and the long tail, served through file ingestion. Run the CSV and scheduled-report route in parallel from the start, since it reaches bespoke and legacy operators that no API adapter will. For delivery, keep Amax Pulse as a thin WordPress client and move normalisation, storage and metrics into a separate multi-tenant service. The WordPress plugin's OAuth, polling and webhook logic carries over into the Stora adapter.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "can this be built?" to "which systems can be reached, and does the demand exist?". Technically, a canonical occupancy core is straightforward and a file-based fallback makes coverage nearly universal. The binding constraints are commercial and unverified: vendor API terms for four of the six main systems, the unknown software of the large chains, and the absence of direct operator demand evidence. A cheap, fast test is available. Fingerprinting operator websites and checking Stora's unit endpoints could be done within days and would show whether an API-led Stora plus Storeganise product covers enough of the market to justify the build, or whether the product must lead with file ingestion.

A second implication concerns positioning. Vendors are shipping their own BI (Storable in late 2025, Kinnovis, Self Storage Manager, Storeganise), so the defensible value is the cross-vendor and cross-site view, plus eventual benchmarking that no single CRM can offer because each sees only its own customers. That argues for pursuing multi-site operators and mixed-system portfolios first, selling a read-only, no-tenant-data pitch, and treating the CRM vendors as partners to approach rather than competitors to avoid.
